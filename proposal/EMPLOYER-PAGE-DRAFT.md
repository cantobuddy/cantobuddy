## Why this exists

The employer portal is **built and working**: invite a helper by link or by a spoken code, an optional
note (*"please practise the Elder Care words"*), a per-helper accuracy % and progress bar, and a
disconnect that leaves the helper's progress untouched.

But `/for-employers` returns **404**, and there is no employer-facing page anywhere on the site. Every
guide is written to the helper. **The feature is invisible and unexplained** — which means the one
mechanism you care about (an employer inviting a helper) has nothing pointing at it.

This is also the person who would pay. Your model is *"learners are never charged; the employer side is
monetised."* So there is currently nothing on the site addressed to your only customer.

### Heading

**Your helper learns Cantonese. Here's how you help.**

*(Alternative: "Help your helper speak Cantonese with your family" — see Q2 in the questions below.)*

### The problem

> Most helpers arrive speaking English and learn Cantonese on the job — usually from the person in
> the household who speaks English least.

If your mother is the one at home all day, she is the one your helper cannot talk to. That is where
the small misunderstandings start: a request repeated three times, a symptom not reported, a medicine
taken at the wrong hour because nobody could confirm it.

*(This is not a new argument — it is already written, in `facebook-hk-groups.html` Post D. It is the
right opening and it is currently nowhere on the site.)*

### What CantoBuddy is

A free Cantonese practice app, built for domestic helpers in Hong Kong. 180 words and phrases for
the situations that actually come up at home — elder care, the kitchen, medication, emergencies —
each with audio, Jyutping, and quizzes.

No sign-up. No cost. Works offline. **Free to your helper, always** — and there is nothing in it to
sell her.

### How to connect with your helper

1. **Sign in** at the employer portal.
2. **Invite her.** You get a link to send, *and* a short code you can read aloud — the codes leave out
   0/O and 1/I so they cannot be misheard over the phone.
3. **Add a note.** One thing, this week. *"Please practise the Elder Care words"* works far better than
   *"please study"*.
4. **She accepts on her own phone.** Sharing is off until she does.

### What you can see — and what you cannot

Say this before they worry about it:

**You can see:** how many quiz attempts she has done, her accuracy (% correct), her total right
answers, a progress bar, her progress per level, and when she last practised.

**You cannot see:**
- her quiz answers, word by word
- her feedback to us
- anything else she does on this site
- anything at all once she disconnects

**She can disconnect at any time, and she keeps every bit of her progress.** The invitation is hers to
accept and hers to end. That is not a setting we might change later — it is the reason a helper can
use this app honestly, and it is written into our privacy policy.

### How to encourage her — without watching her

This is the part that decides whether any of it works.

- **Start small and be specific.** One category this week. A helper given "learn Cantonese" does
  nothing; a helper given "the Elder Care words" opens the app.
- **Invite in the first week, not the first day.** On day one she is learning the household. An app
  request then reads as one more task. Give her a week to settle.
- **Let her own it.** The app is hers. If she asks you not to connect, that is allowed, and the app
  still works completely for her.
- **Say thank you out loud.** You can see when she last practised and how her accuracy is moving.
  Noticing it costs nothing and is the entire mechanism.

**[If you want to recognise progress with a gift, that happens between you and her — the app does not
handle money and never will. See the note at the end.]**

# PART B — the guide (for the employer who will read more)

**Proposed slug:** `getting-your-helper-started-with-cantonese`
**Proposed title:** *Getting Your Helper Started with Cantonese*
**Proposed description:** *Most helpers learn Cantonese from whoever in the household speaks English
least. Here is how to make that easier — and how to connect to her progress without watching her.*

Shorter than the culture guides. Target ~900–1,200 words. Structure:

### 1. Why the person at home all day matters most

The household's English is not evenly distributed. Whoever is home during the day — often an elderly
parent — is the person your helper spends the most time with and can say the least to. Cantonese
therefore matters most in exactly the relationship where it is hardest.

### 2. What to ask her to learn first

Not "start at the beginning". The app has three levels, and the useful thing for your household is
usually:
- **Elder Care, Health & Symptoms, Medicine & Appointments** — if an elderly person is at home
- **Kitchen, Meals & Feeding, Cleaning** — if the day is mostly household
- **Safety** — always

Name one. Let her finish it before naming the next.

### 3. The invitation, and the note

Walk through it, with the text of a good note and a bad note side by side:

> ❌ *"Please study Cantonese every day, it is important."*
> ✅ *"This week, please try the Elder Care words — the ones you'd hear from my mother."*

The difference is that the second one tells her **which words**, and **why**.

### 4. What you will see, and what you will not

Repeat Part A's section, in more detail, including *why* — the helper is a live-in worker whose
employer can see her progress, so a complaint has to be unattributable or it will not be written.
That is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight.

### 5. Encouraging without pressure

- Ask her what she has learned, rather than telling her to practise.
- If she is stuck on a word, ask her to teach it to you. It changes the relationship from supervision
  to shared effort.
- **Recognise progress.** The portal shows how many quizzes she has attempted and how her accuracy is
  moving, and when she last practised. If she has been at it consistently, say so.

### 6. If it does not happen

Some helpers will not want to, and that is a legitimate answer. The app is not a performance
management tool and it is not designed to become one. If she declines, she keeps the app and keeps
every word she has learned.

### FAQ

**Q. Do I need to pay anything?**
A. No. CantoBuddy is free for helpers and always will be. There is no premium tier.

**Q. Can I see what she got wrong?**
A. No — only her overall accuracy. This is deliberate: the app is a place she can be bad at something
without being graded by her employer.

**Q. What if I employ two helpers?**
A. Invite each separately. Each has her own progress and can disconnect independently.

**Q. What if she already uses the app?**
A. Send her the invitation anyway — it connects her existing progress, it does not start her again.

**Q. Can I use this to check she is working?**
A. No. It records learning, only while she chooses to share it, and she can stop at any time.

# ⚠️ The bonus note — read this before anything is published

You chose **(a) recognition.** This draft is written to be **true under (a) and only (a)**. That means:

- ✅ *"Recognise her progress. The portal shows you her attempts and accuracy so you can say thank you
  — and if you want
  to give a gift, that is between you and her."*
- ❌ Nothing anywhere that implies the app pays a bonus, tracks a payout, holds a balance, or ties
  money to app activity.

**Why this constraint is worth keeping even though you chose (a).** Your published privacy policy says:

> *"We do not use your learning activity to make decisions about your employment."*

A bonus calculated from learning activity **is** that decision. So (a) is not just the cheaper option —
it is the one that stays consistent with a promise already made to helpers in four languages. If (b)
ever comes back on the table, that sentence has to be rewritten first, and it is a public promise, not
a config value.

**The single sentence in Part A that carries all of this** is the bracketed one:

> *[If you want to recognise progress with a gift, that happens between you and her — the app does not
> handle money and never will.]*

**"and never will"** — I put that in deliberately, because it makes the claim checkable and it stops
the page being read as a wink toward a future payment feature. **Cut it if you disagree, but say so**,
because it is the line doing the work.
