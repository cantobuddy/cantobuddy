Today — 30 minutes of setup

Do this before you post anything. A post sends people to your profile, and a profile with a blank picture and no link wastes the click.

1
Set the profile picture on every account Use images/profile-avatar.png for all of them. Same picture everywhere means people recognise you across platforms.
2
Write the bio It has to say what the app is and who it's for, in one glance. Use this, or adapt it: 
Free Cantonese for helpers in Hong Kong 🇭🇰
180 words · audio · quizzes
English · Filipino · Bahasa Indonesia · 中文
👇 cantobuddy.com
On Instagram and TikTok the link goes in the bio field (TikTok allows one link once you have an account).
3
Add the cover images Facebook: images/fb-cover.png (1640×624).
YouTube: images/yt-banner.png (2560×1440 — the text sits inside the safe area, so it won't be cropped on TV).
4
Pick a handle Try in this order until one is free on each platform — keeping the same name everywhere is worth a small compromise: 
cantobuddy
cantobuddy.hk
learncantobuddy
cantobuddyapp
5
Make sure the app is installed on your phone Open cantobuddy.com in your phone browser, then "Add to Home Screen". You'll be showing it to people, and it should look like an app, not a web page.
Post from your personal account, but don't make a Facebook Page yet. Pages posting in community groups get treated as advertising and removed. A personal account that contributes genuinely does not. Revisit the Page later, once the app has a following that would follow it.

How to pick which ones to join

Look forAvoid
  • 5,000+ members — enough reach to matter
  • Posts in the last 48 hours — a dead group is dead
  • Mix of topics — questions, advice, sharing, not just job ads
  • Rules that mention promotion — means there IS a way to post
  • Groups that are 90% agency job postings
  • Groups with no posts in weeks
  • Any group whose rules ban links outright — respect it and move on
  • Groups that ask for payment to join

Join 8–10. Post in 3–5. Answer the membership questions honestly — say you're the person who made the app. Being straight about it is what keeps you in.

Read the rules before your first post. Search inside the group for "rules" or check the pinned post. Many allow links only on certain days, or in a specific thread. Following that rule once buys you the right to post again.

Your first post

Open posts.txt on your phone and start with Post 1 in the Filipino groups.

For the very first post, consider leaving the link out. Post the useful content — the four words for elder care, or the 唔該 explainer — with no link at all, and see how the group responds. Then post the link version a few days later to the same group. You'll get more clicks from the second post because the first one earned you credibility.

What to actually expect

Being honest about this, because unrealistic expectations are what make people quit in week two.

WhatRealistic first month
Reactions on a genuinely useful group post20–150 in a 20k-member group
Link clicks from that post5–30
New app users from one good post3–15
Instagram reach on a new accountNear zero for the first 2–3 weeks. This is normal, not failure.
Comments asking a question1–5 per post. These are the valuable ones.
Week one has one job, and it isn't reach. It's finding out which groups accept you, which posts people reply to, and what the app breaks on a real phone. Treat it as research. The numbers come later, and they come from repetition, not from any single post.

When someone comments

This is the part that compounds, and almost nobody does it.

  1. Reply to every comment in the first few hours. Comments push the post back into the feed; likes don't.
  2. If someone asks for a specific word — add it to the app that week, then reply again: "Added it! It's in the Greetings section now." That single exchange is worth more than any hashtag strategy.
  3. If someone says it doesn't work on their phone — ask what phone. That's a real bug you'd otherwise never hear about, and there's a feedback button in the app that lands in your admin inbox.
  4. Never argue. If a group mod tells you to stop, stop, and apologise.

The four ways this fails

1. Posting only links

A post that is just "check out my app" gets removed and gets you banned. Every post in posts.txt teaches something first. Keep it that way.

2. Blasting every group in one day

Facebook notices the same text in five groups within an hour, and so do the moderators. Space them out, vary the wording, and never post the same text twice in the same day.

3. Quitting in week two

The first two weeks look like nothing is happening. That's not failure — that's the baseline. The compounding starts around the point where the same person sees your name for the third time.

4. Not answering comments

A post with 40 comments outranks a post with 40 likes, on every platform. If you have time for one thing, spend it in the comments rather than making another graphic.

And the honest summary: you asked for higher Google ranking and social promotion, and these are the same project. Ranking for "learn cantonese" on a young domain is an authority problem — Google needs to see that people link to you and search for your name. These posts are how that happens. The pillar page is necessary; it is not sufficient.
